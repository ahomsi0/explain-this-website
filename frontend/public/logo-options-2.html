Logo options, round 2

Six directions that avoid the "rounded tile + icon + gradient" template. Each is a single idea, drawn with one weight of line, shown on dark and light at 96, 32 and 16 px with its wordmark. Colours are placeholders: violet is kept so nothing else on the site has to change, but every mark also works in plain black.

A. Highlightthe product is a highlighter for websites
Three lines of a page, one of them swiped with a marker. The wordmark repeats the idea by highlighting "this". Reads at 16 px because the swipe is the only shape with colour.
B. Speech windowa browser that talks
One continuous outline: a browser window whose bottom edge turns into a speech-bubble tail. "Explain" and "website" in a single stroke, no fill, no tile. The three dots keep it recognisably a browser.
C. Squigglewe point at what is wrong
Text lines with a spell-check underline beneath one of them. Everyone knows what the wavy line means. The squiggle is the only accent, so the mark survives being tiny and stays unmistakable.
D. Margin noteannotations in the margin of a page
A square bracket and a dot beside a block of text, the way an editor marks a paragraph. Calm, editorial, nothing like a SaaS tile. The bracket alone works as a favicon.
E. The "e"a letterform, not an icon
A geometric lowercase e with its crossbar running straight through to the edge, so the aperture reads as an open mouth. Letter marks age well and stay ownable. Pairs with the plain wordmark; the e can also sit alone on social avatars.
F. Tooltiphover anything, get an explanation
A cursor with a tooltip above it. The tooltip is the explanation, the cursor is "this". Familiar to anyone who has used a computer, and it has not been used to death by analytics tools.
